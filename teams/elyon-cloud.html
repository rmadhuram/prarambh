Team Deep Dive · Cybersecurity

ELYON CLOUD

Automated middleware that migrates enterprises to post-quantum encryption — "Discover, Migrate, Verify" with zero production code changes.

Mentor: Ms. Padma Pure software Most polished submission in the cohort

What they submitted

Problem
Enterprises run on RSA/ECC encryption that quantum computers will eventually break. Attackers already practice "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later." Manual migration takes years; regulatory deadlines are closing in. Cites DigiCert/Bain 2026: 85% of security leaders expect quantum to break current encryption within a decade; 90% of enterprises have no defense.
Target customers
Regulated, high-sensitivity enterprises: banking & payments, healthcare, defense contractors — facing HNDL exposure, long data-retention laws, and federal PQC compliance mandates.
Solution
An automated middleware wrapper migrating enterprises to NIST FIPS 203/204 quantum-safe encryption with zero production code changes: Discover, Migrate, Verify.

Read: the opposite problem from most of the cohort. The market is real, the deadlines are real, the vocabulary is flawless — this reads like a funded seed deck. The grilling should test whether there's anything underneath the vocabulary: the "zero code changes" claim is technically enormous, and the chosen customers (banks, defense) are the hardest buyers on earth for a student team. Push on depth, not polish.

1The "zero code changes" claim

This is the whole product. Encryption isn't one thing you wrap — it's scattered through TLS, databases, HSMs, signed firmware, tokens, and proprietary protocols.

  • Where does the middleware physically sit? A network proxy? A library shim? A sidecar? Draw it for one concrete case: a bank's TLS traffic, then its database-at-rest encryption.
  • TLS is the easy case — and it's already being solved without you: browsers, Cloudflare, and AWS ship hybrid post-quantum TLS today. If the easy layer is free, your product lives in the hard residue: HSMs, code signing, JWTs, proprietary protocols. Can those be wrapped with zero changes? Honestly?
  • ML-KEM keys and ML-DSA signatures are much larger than RSA/ECC. What breaks — packet sizes, certificate chains, latency budgets — and who fixes it if not production code?
  • "Discover" may be the real product: finding every place crypto is used in an enterprise (a crypto inventory) is a famous unsolved pain. How does your scanner find crypto inside a 20-year-old Java monolith?
  • What have you actually built so far? Show the demo — even wrapping one nginx server counts.

2Selling security to banks and defense — as students

The most trust-gated procurement on earth. Their chosen buyers are the hardest possible first customers.

  • Who is the buyer — title, department, budget line? What is a CISO's first question to a vendor that wants to touch their cryptography?
  • A security vendor needs SOC 2, penetration audits, reference customers, and often years of existence before a bank will pilot it. Defense adds clearances. What's your path around that wall?
  • Is the realistic route a partner channel — selling through a Big 4 / systems integrator running the migration program — rather than direct? Have you talked to one?
  • Should the first customer be a bank at all — or a mid-size Indian SaaS company that needs a PQC-readiness report for its own enterprise customers?

3Competition

PQC migration is a hot, heavily funded space. The question is why this gap exists for a student team.

  • IBM, Thales, Entrust, SandboxAQ, PQShield — plus every cloud provider building PQC natively. Map yourself against them. Who does "automated migration middleware" today, and why will you beat them?
  • If cloud providers make PQC a checkbox on their platforms, what's left for a third-party wrapper? What survives that scenario?
  • What's defensible here: the scanner? migration playbooks per industry? Nothing yet? Say which.

4Urgency & the budget question

"Eventually" is the hardest word in enterprise sales. Deadlines create the market — but whose deadline arrives first?

  • The threat is years out and the survey stats are vendor marketing (DigiCert sells certificates). Which specific regulation, with which date, forces which customer to spend money this fiscal year?
  • HNDL only matters for data that must stay secret for 10+ years. Which data at which customer actually qualifies? Name three examples.
  • Who has a budget line item for PQC migration today — in India specifically? RBI hasn't mandated it. Does your first market have to be the US federal supply chain, and can you reach it?

5Validation

  • How many security leaders have you spoken to — not surveyed, spoken to? What did the most skeptical one say?
  • What would a CISO pay for this year: full migration, or just a credible crypto-inventory report for their board and regulators?
  • Every number in your pitch is from a vendor report. What's your primary evidence — one conversation, one pilot, one letter of intent?

6Scoping & MVP

The wedge is probably the scanner, not the migration. Discovery is sellable as an audit, needs no production trust, and feeds compliance paperwork.

  • Smallest slice: a crypto-discovery scanner that produces a PQC-readiness report (a crypto bill of materials). Ship that alone — would someone pay ₹50k for the report? Who?
  • Two-week test: run the scanner on five large open-source codebases, publish the readiness reports, send them to twenty CISOs on LinkedIn. Count replies. What's the pass mark?
  • The demo that makes the pitch real: one server, hybrid PQC TLS, before/after packet capture on screen. Can you show that at this workshop's final pitch?
  • Sequencing: Discover as the product now, Migrate as the service later, Verify as the compliance upsell. Do you accept that ordering — and if not, what's yours?

7Team & resources

Getting cryptography wrong is worse than not touching it. Credibility is the entire product in security.

  • Who on the team has implemented cryptography — even a TLS integration? Who reviews your crypto decisions?
  • Do you have a security-faculty advisor, and would you submit your wrapper to an external audit before any pilot?
  • Why this problem? What's the team's connection to security — or was this chosen because the market slide looks great? (An honest answer here is fine — but it changes the plan.)
The kill-shot question

"Would you let a startup with no audit, no SOC 2, and no reference customer re-wrap your bank's encryption? Then why will a CISO?"

The honest answer forces the real strategy: start with the scanner and the readiness report — the product a CISO can buy from a student team.

Facts for the mentor table

  • The NIST standards are real and final (Aug 2024): FIPS 203 (ML-KEM, ex-Kyber), FIPS 204 (ML-DSA, ex-Dilithium), FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA). US CNSA 2.0 sets migration timelines into the 2030s.
  • Harvest-Now-Decrypt-Later is a genuine threat model — but only for data whose secrecy must outlive the quantum timeline (health records, state secrets, long-lived financial data).
  • Hybrid post-quantum TLS is already deployed at scale (Chrome, Cloudflare, AWS) — the easy layer is being solved without startups.
  • Crypto discovery/inventory (CBOM) is widely considered the unavoidable first step of any migration — and the most credible wedge product.
  • Funded players in the space include SandboxAQ, PQShield, and the incumbents (IBM, Thales, Entrust, DigiCert).
  • PQC key and signature sizes are substantially larger than RSA/ECC — "zero changes" collides with real packet-size and performance budgets.

Grilling context, not gospel — the students should bring the specific mandate dates and a working demo. That's the point.
