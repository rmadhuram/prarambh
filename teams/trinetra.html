Team Deep Dive · AgriTech

Trinetra

Aeroponic saffron cultivation — growing saffron indoors, anywhere in India, free of Kashmir's geography and climate.

Mentor: Mr. Vijay Trientra entry: Mr. Srinath No software Registered twice (Trinetra / Trientra)

What they submitted

Problem
How can we make saffron cultivation controlled, sustainable and scalable — without letting geography and climate decide where and how much saffron India can produce?
Target customers
"Saffron based industry, middle class customer etc" (Trinetra) · pharma and perfume industries, premium food, beauty and skin, restaurants and hotels, gift buyers, export customers (Trientra)
Solution
Aeroponic farming: a soil-less method where roots are suspended in air and periodically misted with a nutrient-rich solution.

Read: the solution as written is a definition of a technique, not a venture. The grilling below is designed to force the jump from "aeroponics exists" to "we have a business growing and selling saffron." The two entries also give two different customer answers — resolve that first.

1Problem validation

Is anyone actually paying to solve this? "Industry" and "middle class customer" are two different companies.

  • Who exactly suffers because saffron supply is geography-bound? Name one buyer you have spoken to.
  • B2B ingredient supply to pharma/perfume and retail consumer packets are completely different businesses. Which one are you?
  • India imports most of its saffron, and Iranian saffron is far cheaper than Kashmiri. Is the problem availability, price, authenticity, or provenance? Each implies a different product.

2Agronomic & technical feasibility

The hardest grilling zone. Saffron is one of the most demanding crops to move indoors.

  • Have you grown a single saffron flower yet — anywhere, even in a tray?
  • Saffron flowers once a year from a corm and needs a cold vernalization period. How do you get more than one harvest cycle indoors? What is your temperature and light protocol, and where did it come from?
  • Where do the corms come from? Corm supply (Kashmir, Iran, Netherlands) is itself a bottleneck and a cost, and corms multiply slowly. What is your multiplication plan?
  • Yield math: grams of dried saffron per square metre per cycle, realistically. Say a number and defend it.

3Unit economics

Controlled-environment agriculture's graveyard. Indoor farms die on electricity bills; saffron's price per gram is the only reason this might work.

  • Capex per square metre: racks, misting, HVAC, lighting. What does one grow room cost to build?
  • Power cost per gram of saffron produced. Does the math actually close?
  • At what selling price per gram do you break even — and how does that compare to Kashmiri retail and Iranian wholesale prices?
  • What is the payback period on one growing unit?

4Market & competition

  • Kashmiri saffron has a GI tag and a story. Lab-grown saffron has neither. Why does a pharma buyer or a premium consumer choose yours?
  • Indoor saffron is already being attempted in India by multiple startups and hobbyist units. What do you know that they don't?
  • Adulteration is rampant in saffron. Is authenticity and grading (ISO 3632 testing, provenance) actually the bigger opportunity than growing?

5Scoping & MVP

Their claim is "controlled, sustainable and scalable." The riskiest assumption is agronomic, and testing it takes a full growing cycle — months. So what can they learn cheaply, now?

  • What is the smallest version that tests the riskiest assumption? (Likely: one small grow room, one corm batch, one harvest, one paying buyer.)
  • What can you learn in two weeks? For example: pre-sell to a pharma or perfume buyer at your target price, or run the full cost model against published indoor-yield data.
  • Corm to harvest is 6–8 months. What do you do for revenue and learning during the wait?

6Team & resources

  • Who on the team has grown anything? Do you have a horticulture advisor, or access to SRM's agri/biotech labs?
  • This is a capex business, not a laptop business. What is the funding plan for even one pilot room?

7Housekeeping

  • You are registered twice — Trinetra (Mr. Vijay) and Trientra (Mr. Srinath) — with different target-customer answers. One team, one pitch: which version is canonical?
The kill-shot question

"What is your cost per gram — and what does Iranian saffron cost per gram?"

If they can't answer both, everything else is decoration.

Facts for the mentor table

  • Saffron flowers once a year (autumn) from a corm; corms need cold vernalization and multiply slowly.
  • Kashmiri saffron holds a GI tag (granted 2020); its story and grade command the premium.
  • India consumes far more saffron than it grows; imports are dominated by much cheaper Iranian saffron.
  • Kashmiri saffron retails roughly ₹400–600 per gram; adulteration is widespread across the market.
  • ISO 3632 is the grading standard (crocin, picrocrocin, safranal levels).

Ballpark figures for grilling context — have the students bring the precise, current numbers. That's the point.
